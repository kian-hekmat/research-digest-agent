PINNs · Nov 10–13
18 new papers this period · your top 5 below

PINNs are increasingly one step in a pipeline rather than the whole solver. The sharpest example finds nonlinear water waves with a PINN, polishes them with Newton's method, then certifies the result with interval arithmetic and checks the proof in Lean (2611.07395). Others add classical structure to get past training failures: separate velocity and pressure networks for Navier–Stokes (2611.07387), and an architecture built around a domain's holes, where a standard PINN fails outright (2611.05276).

1. Rigorously Certified Neural-Network Computation of Nonlinear Water Waves…
Why read it: a neural network's answer, rigorously certified and machine-checked, which is rare in this field.
Finds Stokes waves, Crapper waves and Wilton ripples from the equations alone, with residuals driven to near round-off. The final solution is certified with interval arithmetic and the verification formalized in Lean.
arxiv.org/abs/2611.07395

2. Projection-based segregated… PINN for incompressible Navier–Stokes
Why read it: a practical fix if your PINNs stall on pressure–velocity coupling.
Splits momentum and pressure correction into separate networks joined by projection steps, and trains in stages. Converges better than standard PINNs on lid-driven cavity flow and Kolmogorov flow.
arxiv.org/abs/2611.07387
